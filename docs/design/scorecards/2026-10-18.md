# Scorecard — 2026-10-18 (pilot)

Build: `7241c4d` · Seats: novice (entry, vocabulary, flow) · operator (density, recovery)

This is the **first** scorecard: there is no previous one to compare against, so nothing can drop
silently yet. It scores all fifteen tabs, plus Evidence as a drill (marked †), at both viewports.

## Method — what is measured and what is judged

Measured, from `scripts/responsive_audit.py` at 1440×900 and 390×844 on the deployed build
(30 pairs; overflow, clipped text, controls under 44px, unnamed controls, meaning-only titles,
DOM size, API calls on load):

- **a11y** — 3 when unnamed = 0, meaning-only titles = 0, clipped = 0, and at most two controls
  under 44px on a phone; 2 when a phone carries 3–10 undersized controls; 1 when it carries more.
- **density** — 3 when the DOM is inside the 6,000-element budget; 2 when it is over by less than
  a quarter; 1 beyond that.

Judged, and therefore reviewable rather than proven: **entry** (is the first screen the answer?),
**vocab** (does it use the operator's words?), **flow** (does the next step follow?), **honesty**
(does it say unknown when it does not know?), **visual** (does hierarchy carry the reading?),
**recovery** (can a mistake be undone or explained?), **tta** (time-to-answer: 3 = on the view,
2 = one click, 1 = a hunt). Those judgments were made by walking the console on this build; the
places where a claim rests on a measurement say so in the notes.

## Hard blockers

None found. No view is a dead end; no view claims a zero it did not measure; every destructive
control is labelled and confirmed (the Sluice trashcan, the rule composer's two-step block, the
settings discard).

## Where the scores are weakest, and why

- **Sluice a11y = 1.** Sixteen controls measure under 44px on a phone — the largest undersized
  set in the console, and the newest surface (status line, glance chips, row verbs). It is the
  next accessibility target; the counts come from the audit, not from inspection.
- **Anomalies density = 2.** 6,144 elements against the 6,000 budget. US-A4 fixed Models by
  paging the points table; Anomalies holds ~100 rows, each with a chip, a disclosure and five
  cells. Paging or virtualising the list is the obvious next move.
- **Entry = 2 on nine views.** Most tabs open with a summary and a control strip; the answer is
  one read away rather than on the first screen. Health is the honest exception: it opens with
  "1 of 9 checks failing", which is exactly the first thing an operator needs.
- **tta = 2 almost everywhere.** That is a considered 2, not a miss: the console is built around
  one drill to the record (the evidence chain), so most answers are one click from the tab. A 3
  means the answer is on the view itself, which only Evidence achieves.
